Foundations · The estrous cycle

The estrous cycle — a ~21-day rhythm

21
days
(range 18–24)
Day 0 · ESTRUS
Proestrus → Estrus (Day 0)
Follicle matures, estrogen peaks → standing heat. Ovulation ~24–30 h after heat onset.
Metestrus → Diestrus (Day 1–17)
Corpus luteum (CL) forms & secretes progesterone — maintains pregnancy or holds the cycle.
Luteolysis (Day 17–18)
If not pregnant, the uterus releases PGF2α → the CL regresses → the next cycle begins.
🐃 BuffaloCycle length similar (~21 d) but estrus is shorter, weaker & often silent — and strongly seasonal. We will return to this.

Foundations · Follicular dynamics

Follicular waves & ovulation

  • Follicles grow in 2–3 waves per cycle (buffalo usually 1–2)
  • Each wave has a dominant follicle; only the last one ovulates
  • The LH surge triggers ovulation ~24–30 h after heat onset
  • The egg is viable only ~6–12 h — timing is everything
Why this matters:
Understanding waves is the basis of synchronization & superovulation — we manipulate which follicles grow and when they ovulate.

Estrus & Heat Detection

The single biggest cause of AI failure worldwide is not bad semen — it is missed heat. For buffalo, this is the central challenge.

10
Estrus · Reading heat

Signs of estrus (heat)

The one sure sign — standing to be mounted
She stands still when mounted by another animal. This is the reference point for AI timing.
Secondary signs
Mounting othersClear mucus dischargeRestlessness / bawling↓ feed & milkSwollen vulvaChin-resting / sniffing
🐃 The buffalo problem
  • Silent / weak heat — few or no visible signs
  • Mostly at night — missed by day-time checks
  • Seasonal — poorer expression in hot months
  • Result: low AI conception from missed heat
→ This is exactly the gap sensors & timed-AI close (Parts 3 & 5).
Estrus & heat detection

Estrus · Detection methods

How we catch heat — low-tech to high-tech

👁️ Visual observation
3× daily, ≥20 min, quiet times (early AM / late PM). Free but labour-heavy & misses night heat.
🎨 Tail paint / chalk
Rubbed-off paint = she was mounted. Cheap, effective aid to observation.
🐂 Teaser / heat-mount detectors
Vasectomized teaser bull or pressure-activated patches (KaMaR).
🚶 Pedometers / activity
Heat ↑ walking 2–4×. Automated & objective — good for silent heat.
📡 Rumen & body sensors
Temperature + rumination + activity → heat alerts, day or night. Best fit for buffalo.
🩺 Ultrasound / palpation
Confirms follicle/CL & pregnancy — the vet's cross-check.

Estrus · Timing of insemination

Timing: the AM / PM rule

Sperm need ~6 h in the tract to become fertile (capacitation) and live ~24 h; the egg lives only ~6–12 h. So we inseminate late in heat / just before ovulation.

Heat seen in the MORNING → inseminate that EVENING
Heat seen in the EVENING → inseminate next MORNING
Rule of thumb: AI about 12 hours after first standing heat; ovulation follows ~24–30 h after heat onset.

~24 h

Estrus · Synchronization

Estrus synchronization — scheduling the herd

Instead of waiting for each animal, we bring a whole group into heat together — enabling planned, batch AI.

PGF2α (single / double)
Destroys the CL → heat in 2–5 days. Simple & cheap; needs an active CL (cycling animals).
Progesterone device (CIDR / PRID)
Intravaginal insert mimics the CL, then withdrawn. Works on non-cycling / silent buffalo & anestrus.
Ovsynch (→ Fixed-time AI)
GnRH → PGF2α → GnRH controls ovulation itself → AI by the clock, no heat detection needed.
🐃 BuffaloProgesterone + Ovsynch-type protocols are the practical answer to silent, seasonal heat — we detail the timeline next.

Semen: collection → freezing → storage

AI · Handling & thawing — where fertility is won or lost

Straw handling: the details that decide conception

  • Work fast in the tank neck — lift the canister no higher than the frost line, ≤10 s exposure
  • Thaw in a water bath at 35–37 °C for 30–45 s — never in a pocket or by air
  • Dry the straw, load a pre-warmed gun, protect from cold shock & sunlight
  • Inseminate within ~15 minutes of thawing
  • One straw thawed at a time
35–37°C
thaw temperature · 30–45 s
−196°C
tank storage · avoid re-cooling a thawed straw
Cold shock & slow thawing kill sperm — good semen fails from poor handling.

AI · Technique & deposition site

The recto-vaginal technique

  • Gloved hand in the rectum grips the cervix; the gun passes through the vagina
  • Manipulate the cervix onto the gun — thread through its rings
  • Deposit semen in the body of the uterus (site AI), just past the cervix
  • Deposit slowly; withdraw gently; minimize stress & trauma
Golden rule
"Quick, clean, gentle." A calm animal & clean pass protect the pregnancy you are trying to create.
Deposition site: body of the uterus (not the vagina, not deep in a horn).

Part Four
Embryo Transfer & IVF

If AI multiplies the sire, embryo transfer multiplies the elite female — turning one exceptional donor into a whole generation.

22
Embryo transfer · The concept

Why embryo transfer (MOET & IVF)?

A cow normally gives ~1 calf a year. With embryo technology, one elite donor can produce dozens of calves a year — carried by ordinary recipient females.

AMOET — superovulate the donor, AI, flush embryos ~day 7
BOPU–IVF — aspirate eggs from live donors, fertilize in the lab
CTransfer fresh, or freeze / vitrify for later & for export
🐃 For scarce elite buffalo

Superior river-buffalo females are rare & valuable. ET/IVF is the fastest way to propagate them nationally — and to conserve endangered genetics.

Honest note: buffalo superovulate less predictably than cattle — OPU–IVF is often the better route.

Embryo transfer · Recovery & evaluation

Embryo recovery & grading

  • Day 7 non-surgical flush — a catheter in the uterine horn, warm media collects the embryos
  • Search under the microscope; identify stage: morula → blastocyst
  • Grade 1 (excellent) → 4 (degenerate) on shape, colour & cell compactness
  • Only good embryos are transferred or frozen

OPU–IVF: making embryos in the laboratory

1OPU — ultrasound-guided egg aspiration from live donors (repeatable, even pregnant/young)
2IVM — oocyte maturation in culture (~24 h)
3IVF — fertilize with (sexed) semen
4IVC — culture ~7 days to blastocyst
5Transfer fresh, or vitrify at −196 °C
Advantage for buffalo: OPU sidesteps their poor superovulation response.

Embryo transfer · Choosing the calf's sex

Sexed semen & sexed embryos

To grow a dairy or breeding herd, you want females. Flow-sorted sexed semen (~90% female) and sexed IVF embryos let you plan the sex of the calf.

  • Faster herd expansion — more replacement heifers
  • More heifers born to your best females via IVF
  • Fewer unwanted male calves in a dairy system

Rumen bolus sensor, inside the animal
Smart farm · Sensor-based detection

A sensor that never sleeps — so it catches silent heat

A rumen bolus continuously measures inner body temperature (±0.01 °C), rumination & activity — reading estrus and health from inside the animal, 24 hours a day.

🔥 Heat alerts, day or night
Activity + temperature flag estrus even when signs are invisible — the buffalo breakthrough.
📅 Insemination window
Tells you the ideal AI hour per animal — precision timing.
🐄 Calving prediction
Alerts ~15–30 h before calving — fewer losses.
❤️ Early illness
Fever & metritis flagged days early — protects fertility.
